To meaningfully reduce vehicle miles traveled (VMT), expand housing supply, and build neighborhood-level affordability across Minneapolis, the city needs to move beyond high-level zoning declarations and tackle the granular regulatory, structural, and infrastructure bottlenecks.

Here are four targeted ordinances designed to directly achieve those three goals:

---

### 1. The Missing Middle Density & Dimensional Parity Act

* **Primary Target:** Add more housing across high-opportunity neighborhoods (Southwest, Nokomis, Northeast) that resisted multi-unit growth under initial 2040 reforms.
* **Problem:** While the *Minneapolis 2040* plan legalized triplexes citywide, developers frequently found them financially unviable because the city retained restrictive Floor Area Ratio (FAR), setback, lot coverage, and height limits originally designed for single-family homes. A triplex had to fit inside the exact envelope of an older single-family house.
* **Legislative Mechanisms:**
* **Graduated FAR by Unit Count:** Increase base FAR from ~0.5–0.7 up to 1.1 for 3- to 6-unit buildings by-right, giving multi-family structures the interior square footage needed for 2- and 3-bedroom family units.
* **Single-Stair Reform (Up to 6 Stories):** Align the Minneapolis building safety code with modern international standards by permitting single-stair egress for buildings up to 6 stories. This unlocks construction on narrow 40-to-50-foot infill lots, increases cross-ventilation, lowers construction costs per square foot, and avoids wasteful double-loaded corridor footprints.
* **Permit Fee Waivers for Infill:** Fully waive city plan-review and hookup fees for any 2-to-6 unit infill project replacing a dilapidated structure or building on an underutilized vacant lot.



---

### 2. The 15-Minute Neighborhood Commercial Legalization Act

* **Primary Target:** Drastically reduce Vehicle Miles Traveled (VMT) for daily routine trips.
* **Problem:** Most car trips in Minneapolis are not long commutes—they are short, 1-to-2 mile drives for basic errands (groceries, dry cleaning, coffee, prescriptions) because residential neighborhoods prohibit neighborhood-serving commercial uses.
* **Legislative Mechanisms:**
* **Corner Store & "Boutique Retail" By-Right:** Allow small-scale, quiet commercial spaces (up to 1,800 sq ft) on all street corners in residential districts by-right. Permitted uses would include bodegas/grocers, bakeries, cafes, daycare centers, and repair workshops.
* **Adaptive Reuse of Historic Commercial Storefronts:** Legalize the grandfathered reactivation of any former corner grocery or commercial space in residential zones without requiring a conditional use permit or public zoning variance hearings.
* **Micro-Delivery Hubs:** Authorize cargo-bike and electric micro-van transfer depots in industrial-commercial buffer zones to replace heavy delivery truck traffic in dense residential streets.



---

### 3. Transit-Oriented Density & Parking Decoupling Ordinance

* **Primary Target:** Lower rent costs, eliminate hidden car subsidies, and shift trips to Metro Transit lines.
* **Problem:** In 2021, Minneapolis eliminated minimum parking requirements, but developers often bundle parking spaces into residential rents, forcing car-free tenants to subsidize off-street stalls (which cost $40,000–$60,000 per space to build underground).
* **Legislative Mechanisms:**
* **Mandatory Parking Unbundling:** Require all multi-family buildings with 10 or more units to lease parking stalls completely separately from residential leases. If a resident does not own a car, their monthly housing cost immediately drops by the parking rate ($150–$250/month).
* **Transit Priority Upzoning:** Enact automatic by-right zoning bonuses (up to 8–10 stories, 0 parking maximums) within 1/4 mile of all METRO Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) stations (e.g., A, B, C, D, and E Lines) and LRT stops.
* **Surface Parking Conversion Tax Credit:** Create an expedited administrative track and tax abatement schedule for property owners who convert strip-mall surface parking lots along major commercial corridors (Lake St, Nicollet Ave, Broadway Ave) into mixed-use, mixed-income housing.



---

### 4. The Complete Green Streets & Micro-Mobility Equity Act

* **Primary Target:** Make walking, transit, and rolling safer, more convenient, and faster than short driving trips.
* **Problem:** Paint-only bike lanes and icy sidewalks discourage non-motorized trips for children, elderly residents, and winter commuters.
* **Legislative Mechanisms:**
* **Permanent Concrete Separation:** Amend the Minneapolis Complete Streets policy to require physically protected barriers (concrete curbs, bollards, or raised grade) for any new or resurfaced bike route on streets with speed limits of 25 mph or higher.
* **Dedicated Bus-Only Lanes with Camera Enforcement:** Convert the curbside travel lanes on high-frequency transit corridors (e.g., Hennepin, Chicago, Central, Lake) into dedicated bus and emergency-vehicle lanes during peak hours, pairing with state legislative lobbying for automated bus-lane enforcement.
* **Municipal Sidewalk Snow Clearing:** Expand city-funded sidewalk clearing across high-pedestrian corridors and school walk zones. Winter mobility is an equity issue: snow-packed pedestrian ramps force transit riders into car-dominated travel lanes or keep mobility-impaired residents trapped at home.



---

## Legislative Matrix

| Bill / Ordinance | Direct Housing Impact | Direct VMT Impact | Direct Affordability Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Missing Middle Parity** | Unlocks 3–6 unit infill across low-density wards | Densifies existing street grids; shortens trip lengths | Cheaper wood-frame construction; increases starter home options |
| **15-Minute Neighborhoods** | Integrates live-work and upper-floor apartments | Eliminates car trips for daily errands and food runs | Lowers car-dependency costs ($10k+/yr per household) |
| **Transit Density & Unbundling** | Concentrates thousands of units on transit spines | Shifting primary commutes to high-capacity rail/BRT | Reduces base rent by $150–$250/mo for non-drivers |
| **Complete Green Streets** | Focuses growth around accessible right-of-ways | Safely switches short car trips to bike, scooter, and bus | Provides free, reliable active transit infrastructure year-round |
